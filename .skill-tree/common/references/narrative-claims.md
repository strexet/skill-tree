# Narrative claims and attributed statements

Record consequential claims when authority, a route gate, a character's knowledge or a potential contradiction requires an exact statement. Ordinary descriptive prose can stay in the record. A claim uses a stable local claim ID and a source record ID with an accepted revision or a selected draft fingerprint. A presentation beat is a source of access, while the supporting record establishes the authority and scope.

| Kind | Meaning | Review consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Established fact | The author accepts the assertion for an explicit scope | Compare with other established assertions in overlapping scope |
| Attributed statement | A named speaker claims, believes or remembers the assertion | Check attribution and knowledge. Compare evidence before interpreting a conflict |
| Rumor | A claim circulates through identified channels | Keep spread and reliability separate |
| Deliberate ambiguity | The work intentionally withholds a determination | Preserve supported readings and any fixed boundaries |
| Open decision | The author has yet to decide an important assertion or scope | Retain the question and hold dependent acceptance |

Use a concise statement with subject and proposition. Scope lists continuity, period, place and route or outcome. Use an explicit unknown value for unresolved scope and a declared universal value only when the author establishes universal scope. Include the exact origin and optional supporting sources. A draft can contain an established claim proposed for review, while editorial approval remains controlled by the selected snapshot.

Compare facts only after establishing scope overlap. A witness can sincerely report an inaccurate belief. The holder's evidence and the player presentation remain separate records. When scope or supporting evidence is absent, record uncertainty and identify the consequential question. Escalate an in-scope contradiction between two established facts with both source locators.

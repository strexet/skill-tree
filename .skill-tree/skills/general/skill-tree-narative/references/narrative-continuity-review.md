# Scoped Continuity Review

## Establish the comparison

Resolve the exact work, continuity, period, place, legal outcomes, accepted pins and named draft revisions. Identify which claims overlap in scope. Record uncertain scope separately. Review event order, causal preconditions, participant availability, knowledge and cross-work relationships through the relevant routes. Use [knowledge and disclosure traces](narrative-disclosure.md) where a line relies on a prior source.

## Classify and record a finding

| Classification | Appropriate finding |
| --- | --- |
| Confirmed contradiction | Two authoritative claims disagree within the same scope, or the current behavior violates a verified requirement. |
| Knowledge or availability defect | A holder asserts an unsupported fact, or an action requires an absent participant or unavailable object. |
| Insufficient evidence | Scope or source is missing, so a material conflict cannot yet be established. |
| Intentional ambiguity | Attributed beliefs or interpretations can coexist under the current authoring intent. |
| Editorial suggestion | A compatible change may improve pacing, clarity or dramatic support. |

Record finding ID, exact compared sources and versions, relevant work and route, beat, affected scope, classification and proposed resolution. Cite both sides of a conflict or the condition and absent evidence that establish a route defect. State owner questions only where the author must decide. Compare a compatible fix with an intentional extension or scoped retcon where useful. Identify affected records, outcomes and pins before proposing a retcon.

## Recheck a revision

Re-evaluate the changed source and affected routes. Resolve or dismiss a finding only with a traceable reason. An unrelated uncertain claim remains open outside the scoped conclusion. Preserve review and author approval as separate evidence. Leave runtime behavior and player understanding unqualified unless appropriate observations exist.

In the synthetic Ridge case, the established short at 06:12 can coexist with Ilya's storm attribution and another witness's earlier-failure belief. A draft naming 06:40 as the first short contradicts the established 06:12 event if both refer to the same occurrence and scope. A skip-route line giving Ilya the exact time requires an earlier available source. A revised line can refer only to the visible heater cable without altering the event.

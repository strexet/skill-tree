# Focused Game Design playtest

Use the [human observation protocol](narrative-human-playtest.md) for consent, a neutral starting instruction, first uncoached choice, intervention, open follow-up questions and the participant's own words. Use the [shared record contract](game-design-records.md) to pin the observation and the decision. Game Design leads a comprehension, discoverability or experience question. Balance leads a comparison of effective settings, adoption or comparable groups. A single question and a bounded observation can inform a decision.

## Prepare one decision

Name the question, decision owner, intended experience and outcome that would change the recommendation. Pin the selected design, scenario and build or identify a paper exercise. Record mode, entry state, opportunity to act, prior familiarity, device, language, accessibility and assist settings that matter. Match relevant conditions and exposure when comparing variants. For a first-discovery question use fresh players or an untouched variant, and record order so that earlier attempts cannot be mistaken for a first exposure.

Define a visible action, the opportunity that makes an attempt eligible, and the clock, start and stopping condition. Record the time and content of later coaching or intervention. Retain all attempts with inclusion or exclusion reasons. Count a first choice made before intervention under the recorded conditions. Exclude coached actions, rehearsed paths and runs without an opportunity from an unaided first-choice measure. Keep eligible counts and raw observations even when a comparison remains open.

| Question | Observable outcome and competing explanation |
| --- | --- |
| Comprehension | Compare the player's stated intent and expected outcome with the observed action. Check whether a missed cue, misunderstood instruction or unclear system response could explain the action. |
| Route discovery | Observe the first cue seen, first choice and progress before coaching. A rehearsal can establish reachability under the supplied path. A fresh attempt with the ordinary camera distinguishes route visibility from failure to understand the goal. |
| Game feel | Record the control, cue, intended input and perceived feedback. Compare a known-action trace on the selected device with a neutral cue trial to distinguish the effective input window from recognition or feedback. |
| Adoption after tuning | Record first and repeated eligible opportunities, deliberate uses, alternatives and the effective setting. Compare matched exposure and starting state before attributing a change to the tuning value. |

## Interpret the evidence

Keep the observed sequence and participant account distinct from the facilitator prompt, modeled prediction, competing hypotheses and design-owner decision. A technical pass establishes the checked behavior under the recorded conditions. A simulated persona predicts what the modeled policy would do. Claims about comprehension, enjoyment or perceived response need relevant participant observations. Creative acceptance needs the decision owner's recorded choice. Leave the corresponding conclusion open when the target, comparison or participant evidence is missing.

State the smallest follow-up that distinguishes the remaining explanations. Name one recipient, the exact revision and the expected observation. A cue visibility or affordance question can go to UI, an audible signal to audio, an access barrier to accessibility, translated wording to localization, and an effective input or writer discrepancy to the implementation owner. Keep the next design decision with the design owner and report any uncovered participant or setting scope.

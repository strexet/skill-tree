# Factions with incentives and internal positions

Use a faction card to design a collective actor whose resources and disagreements create actionable situations.

1. Record public aims, practical incentives, authority and material resources. Keep public claims attributed. List the means of influence and limits on enforcement.
2. Identify at least two internal positions. Explain why each member stays, what each member fears losing, and which obligations override faction preference.
3. Map external relationships and one actionable player interaction. For the same choice, compare how two members react based on evidence, information and personal constraints.
4. Mark inferred responses as proposed. Make a factual claim only when a selected accepted source establishes the claim.
5. Review a faction-wide label for unsupported generalization. Preserve divergent positions unless a setting rule or accepted event explains agreement.

A useful card records who can change access, prices, information or support. Descriptive ideology alone cannot explain an unavailable resource or a particular member response.

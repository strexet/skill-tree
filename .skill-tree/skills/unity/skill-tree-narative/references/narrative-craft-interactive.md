# Interactive Structure Patterns

The cards use synthetic examples. Record design intent and observed results separately.

<a id="convergence-differences"></a>

## Convergence with retained differences

Playbook position `27.16`. Technique.

- **Selection.** Branch count is expensive but previous actions need consequences after paths rejoin.
- **Game application.** Use a common scene with route-specific evidence, obligations, resources or available actions. Keep the retained distinctions in a small state contract.
- **Intended effect.** A player can recognize which earlier decision matters in the shared scene.
- **Misuse risk.** A scene that erases every distinction may turn branches into cosmetic labels.
- **Example.** Two routes reach the harbor hearing. One brings a tide diagram and can challenge timing. The other brings a willing witness and can question motive.
- **Evidence.** Compare legal actions and evidence in the shared scene for both route states. Verify neither route receives the missing source without a cause.

<a id="fail-forward"></a>

## Continue after failure

Playbook position `27.17`. Technique.

- **Selection.** A failure can change the player's position instead of ending the sequence.
- **Game application.** Introduce a new playable situation with meaningful cost, pressure or reduced control. Preserve a legible difference from ordinary success.
- **Intended effect.** A player understands that failure changed the situation and that a response remains available.
- **Misuse risk.** Granting the same benefit after failure under a different label removes the promised consequence.
- **Example.** A failed nighttime crossing leads to detention. The player can still negotiate passage but loses the choice to carry uninspected cargo.
- **Evidence.** Trace success and detention to distinct actions and outcomes. Verify the lost cargo option remains unavailable after failure.

<a id="multiple-inference-paths"></a>

## Multiple paths to an inference

Playbook position `27.18`. Technique.

- **Selection.** A necessary inference depends on one easily missed clue.
- **Game application.** Provide independent evidence routes with compatible conclusions and distinct discovery experiences. Keep optional texture separate from required understanding.
- **Intended effect.** A player can reach the required conclusion without following one prescribed investigation order.
- **Misuse risk.** Two clues behind the same optional gate are not independent, and reaching a conclusion screen is not evidence of actual inference.
- **Example.** A drainage diagram explains the gate timing. A crew schedule and an observed floodmark independently support the same timeline if the diagram is skipped.
- **Evidence.** Walk the route without the diagram and verify usable evidence appears before the decision. Ask how the participant reached the conclusion.

<a id="learn-vary-apply"></a>

## Learn, vary and apply

Playbook position `27.19`. Technique.

- **Selection.** A meaningful mechanical decision depends on a skill the player has never exercised.
- **Game application.** Introduce a safe action, vary the conditions, then ask for meaningful application. Make success or failure express a consequence of the story.
- **Intended effect.** A player recognizes what prior practice can change during a pressured decision.
- **Misuse risk.** A demonstration that hides the relevant control or changes the rules during the final scene does not prepare the player.
- **Example.** A player tests a pressure latch at the shop, operates a sticky latch during maintenance and later chooses whether to use the technique on a flooding gate.
- **Evidence.** Verify the same core control and pressure cue appear during practice and application. Check the final outcome depends on the action performed.

<a id="freedom-direction"></a>

## Alternation of freedom and direction

Playbook position `27.20`. Technique.

- **Selection.** A sequence has uniform pacing and the story benefits from shifting player autonomy.
- **Game application.** Alternate a phase for exploring clues or relationships with a more directed scene where the discovered context matters.
- **Intended effect.** A player experiences a change in pressure while retaining context and meaningful options.
- **Misuse risk.** Forced direction can erase a promised investigation choice. Unstructured exploration can obscure the next actionable goal.
- **Example.** The player searches the harbor at leisure before a timed evacuation hearing. Optional discoveries provide distinct questions in the hearing.
- **Evidence.** Trace several exploration orders into the hearing and verify the objective and available questions remain legible. Observe attention and pace separately.

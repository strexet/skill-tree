# Dialogue and Beat Revision

Start with the [scene and beat design workflow](narrative-scene-design.md) and preserve the selected scene function unless the author chooses a new function. Pin exact dialogue, outline, participant and outcome sources. Inspect alternate route states before assuming the same line is available everywhere.

## Diagnose the exchange

For a consequential line or interaction, identify speaker objective, tactic, obstacle, leverage, speech pattern, stated information and supported subtext. Check where the speaker acquired the stated information and which route supplies the evidence. Read the following beat for a visible response, a new opportunity or a cost. Exposition can remain where direct explanation serves the scene and the speaker can reasonably know the fact.

## Compare edits

Offer a focused revision and a larger alternative when both serve a clear purpose. A focused revision can give a request a believable obstacle or show a consequence with fewer lines. A larger alternative can shift a disclosure to an observed action, environment or another speaker. Compare both alternatives against voice, access to knowledge, production cost, affected beats and later dependencies. Recommend one based on author intent and evidence.

Write choices as actions the player can actually perform. Compare the offered label, immediate character response, state change and subsequent effect. Mark changed knowledge or outcomes for the runtime handoff. Do not add a disclosure to an NPC who missed the source or change an action behind a compatible-looking label.

## Worked comparison

In the harbor inspection, the captain needs reliable evidence and the apprentice needs access. A focused exchange starts with “Name the berth.” The captain follows the apprentice's unsupported answer with “Bring a receipt, or follow me to the quay.” The tactic changes after the refusal and the supervised action remains available.

A larger alternative makes the captain compare the receipt against an illuminated berth register. The visual check reveals the mismatch while leaving the player a choice of supervised access. The visual approach costs a new interaction and changes a later disclosure. The focused revision suits a small dialogue-only edit.

# Choice and Value Patterns

The cards use synthetic examples. Record design intent and observed results separately.

<a id="value-conflict"></a>

## Conflict of values

Playbook position `27.12`. Technique.

- **Selection.** A decision has one obvious good outcome and an alternative without an understandable reason.
- **Game application.** Give each action a legitimate interest, information available before selection and a visible sacrifice. Keep costs unequal where the story justifies the difference.
- **Intended effect.** A player can explain what each alternative protects and sacrifices.
- **Misuse risk.** An empty choice label cannot establish meaningful values even if the game stores two flags.
- **Example.** Allocate the last clean water to a clinic or the crews keeping the flood barrier closed. Both choices protect lives and leave a different group at risk.
- **Evidence.** Trace each option to a visible result and ask a participant to explain the tradeoff before the outcome. Compare an option with no protected interest as a negative case.

<a id="temptation-price"></a>

## Temptation and price

Playbook position `27.13`. Technique.

- **Selection.** An offered reward is flat because the benefit or sacrifice has no visible consequence.
- **Game application.** Name the desired benefit, the actual price and when the player can learn the risk. Tie the sacrifice to a route or relationship state.
- **Intended effect.** A player understands why the offer is attractive and what taking the offer puts at risk.
- **Misuse risk.** A price that the story immediately refunds can make an apparently consequential choice empty.
- **Example.** A smuggler offers the signal lamp in exchange for the last forged pass. The lamp enables the crossing, but surrendering the pass closes an anonymous exit.
- **Evidence.** Verify the crossing opens and the exit closes for the same decision. Check a version that restores the pass without cause as an adverse case.

<a id="delayed-consequence"></a>

## Delayed consequence

Playbook position `27.14`. Technique.

- **Selection.** Earlier choices disappear after a reward screen and no later event recognizes the decision.
- **Game application.** Store the relevant earlier decision and provide a later beat with understandable causal feedback. Keep the later effect within the stated scope.
- **Intended effect.** A player can connect a changed relationship or opportunity to a past decision.
- **Misuse risk.** An unexplained global change can look like a random penalty or a missing branch.
- **Example.** Sharing a spare rope earns a deckhand's trust. Several scenes later the deckhand volunteers access to the maintenance records.
- **Evidence.** Compare equivalent routes with and without the spare rope and verify the cause is legible at the later disclosure.

<a id="renewed-value-test"></a>

## Renewed value test

Playbook position `27.15`. Technique.

- **Selection.** A stable character needs pressure without requiring a forced change of identity.
- **Game application.** Return to the same principle with a higher or different cost. Preserve the option to hold, compromise or change the value as appropriate.
- **Intended effect.** A player understands what persistence costs and why a repeated principle matters.
- **Misuse risk.** Repeating the same choice with equivalent stakes adds little. Growth is not mandatory.
- **Example.** A warden first shares a public warning at no cost. Later the warden can share a more complete warning by losing an official appointment.
- **Evidence.** Compare the costs and available outcomes of both tests. Check that a character who persists can remain coherent and dramatically useful.

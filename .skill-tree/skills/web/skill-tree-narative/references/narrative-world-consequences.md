# Trace consequences of a world-rule change

Review a rule delta before expanding lore. Start with exact earlier and candidate clauses, version or proposal identifiers, actor scope, place, period and route. Distinguish a changed physical constraint from a social or institutional response.

## Build a causal trace

1. List changed inputs, costs, availability and outputs. Record where the rule is actually invoked and where an existing route remains outside the delta.
2. Derive necessary consequences by applying the candidate clause to a concrete action. Name the prerequisites and the prior result.
3. List plausible responses from specific actors and institutions. Test the resources, access, authority, incentives, knowledge and time needed for each response. Two actors can respond differently to the same necessary change.
4. Mark new creative options separately from facts and supported responses. Identify affected scenes, character tactics, faction access and mechanics with exact source links.
5. Ask up to three material questions. Present options, consequence scope and a recommendation. Mark missing costs, enforcement powers or information as assumptions until evidence resolves the gaps.

## Review output

| Record | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Delta | Earlier clause, candidate clause and applicable scope |
| Causal path | Clause → action → necessary outcome or changed constraint |
| Plausible adaptation | Actor, incentive, available resources, alternatives and uncertainty |
| Story option | Proposed event or rule extension, affected material and decision owner |
| Unaffected | Route or record with reason for exclusion |
| Decision | Open question, alternatives, tradeoffs, recommendation and held edits |

For a candidate insulated heater lead, a sound low-draw route can avoid the short. One institution can prioritize repairs, while another can maintain an isolation rule until inspection. Neither institutional policy follows automatically from insulation. A proposed special material cost needs a source. Record an unknown cost rather than inventing a restriction.

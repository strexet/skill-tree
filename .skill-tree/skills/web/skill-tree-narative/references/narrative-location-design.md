# Locations with playable affordances

Define what a location does before listing decoration. Use an exact work, time and route scope. Mark new spatial facts as proposals until approved.

| Field | Design question |
| --- | --- |
| Purpose and rhythm | What services operate here, when does access or activity change, and why does the player visit? |
| Occupants and resources | Who controls, depends on or contests available space and supplies? |
| Access and landmarks | What physical paths, observation points and encounter spaces exist? Which route rules apply? |
| Player verbs | What can the player examine, traverse, operate, negotiate or confront? |
| Knowledge | What can observation show without exposition? What remains author-only or needs another route? |
| Change and feedback | What visible feature changes after a decision, and what caused the change? |

Sketch the opening and resulting locations using only locations relevant to the current episode. Separate physical barriers, gameplay gates and social restrictions. A locked door requires a stated lock or authority. A read-only observation point can still support play when a destination remains inaccessible.

Walk one viable route and one denied route. State where each route becomes available, what information the player receives and how a later change affects navigation or meaning. Preserve an explicit blockage when the design lacks an alternative.

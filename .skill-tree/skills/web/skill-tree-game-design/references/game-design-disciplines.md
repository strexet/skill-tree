# Choose a design exercise and owner

Select the requested decision and deliverable before choosing an exercise. Identify direct player control or an autonomous trigger, the effective clock, time pressure, planning horizon and participating roles. A turn, active input window and return after an absence need different observations. Open the matching procedure and any evidence needed for the selected question. Use the [shared records contract](game-design-records.md) to bind revisions and owners.

State a falsifiable result and a concrete counterexample for a level, puzzle, generator, cooperative role or assist. Request specialist input for a named dependency, such as a sightline, translated clue, input timing or accessible cue. Record the specific missing observation and owner. The selected design exercise can finish with a project document.

## Direct requests

| Request | Lead operation | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Develop a game premise | Game Design | Brief, distinct playable activities and one prototype question |
| Edit a GDD | Game Design | Scoped rule revision with a normal case and a boundary |
| Combine mechanics from a deck | Mechanics | Comparable placements, costs and rejection conditions |
| Find a place for a mechanic | Mechanics | An applicable and an unsuitable placement with a distinguishing condition |
| Maintain a mechanics atlas | Mechanics | Verified and suspected uses, coverage gaps and impact references |
| Investigate balance | Balance | Reproducible comparison or measurement plan |
| Prepare and interpret a playtest | Game Design for a design choice, Balance for a parameter or population comparison | A scenario, observation and decision criterion |
| Prepare a cross-domain handoff | Game Design | Exact design revision and the affected recipient and check |

## Design exercise coverage

| Research exercise | Lead | Minimum input | Output | Independent check |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Translate a player fantasy into activity | Game Design | Intended experience, audience and constraint | Two activities with visible responses | A reader predicts distinct actions or results under a shared situation. |
| 2. Design interview | Game Design | One unresolved experience choice | A decision, alternatives and affected rule | The answer changes a consequence the player can observe. |
| 3. Generate by subtraction | Game Design | Current loop, production constraint and removable feature | Smaller prototype and surviving choice | The proposed loop preserves the selected experience under the same trial. |
| 4. Transfer between genres | Mechanics | Source rule, target activity and a limit | Transferable trigger, result and candidate placement | A contrasting context makes the transfer unsuitable. |
| 5. Compare alternatives | Game Design | Goal and common prototype budget | Two feasible activity variants and comparison | A reader can choose a case that distinguishes the variants at comparable cost. |
| 6. Map available information | Game Design | Actor knowledge, system truth and reveal event | Before-and-after knowledge map | An actor with an insufficient clue cannot derive the required inference. |
| 7. Identify unproductive repetition | Game Design | A recorded or described segment and intended pace | Keep or remove decisions for actions and waits | Shortening a candidate interval preserves any intended recovery or anticipation. |
| 8. Reverse-engineer an episode | Mechanics | Versioned clip or trace and observed transition | Candidate rules, assumptions and a discriminating case | A second trace can disprove one explanation. |
| 9. Map learning | Game Design | Rule and first required use | Demonstration, safe practice, retry and transfer | A fresh situation reveals whether the learner can apply the rule. |
| 10. Design a puzzle | Game Design | Goal, available clues and rule constraints | Deduction graph, minimal solution and hint sequence | A missing clue breaks the expected inference. A hint preserves the remaining deduction. |
| 11. Construct a tactical encounter | Game Design | Known actions, eligible targets and start state | Encounter sequence and recovery route | A legal bypass or failed condition checks whether both taught abilities are needed. |
| 12. Plan rhythm and silence | Game Design | Event sequence and intended tension | Pace, cues and recovery timeline | Observe the next available action before and after moving one event. |
| 13. Size a space around an action | Game Design | Input, approach speed, sightline and required clearance | Blockout dimensions and a decision point | An obstructed or undersized boundary fails the specified traversal or cue. |
| 14. Investigate game feel | Game Design | Action, cue and timing report | Cue, input, confirmation and recovery trace | A changed cue or input window separates candidate explanations. Observe the target device for perceived response. |
| 15. Budget attention | Game Design | Concurrent cues at a named decision | Priority and timing proposal | Compare recognition when a competing cue appears earlier or later. |
| 16. Design independent assists | Game Design | Intended choice, an access barrier and available controls | A separately adjustable rule, input or cue | A boundary case preserves choice under the assist. A changed action or hidden information falsifies equivalence. |
| 17. Design systemic co-op | Game Design | Roles, knowledge, actions and delay condition | Per-role decisions, release rule and recovery | An absent actor or single expert tests whether the other participant retains a meaningful choice. |
| 18. Include spectators and eliminated players | Game Design | Entry, removal and waiting conditions | Participation or return route | An eliminated participant can identify the promised next contribution before re-entry. |
| 19. Treat the world as a system | Game Design | Two world rules and a shared trigger | Causal trace and narrative handoff | Reversing the order or removing a prerequisite changes the expected episode. |
| 20. Catalog interesting mistakes | Mechanics | Observed errors and resulting actions | Cited mistakes, recovery and candidate support | The reviewed trace distinguishes a new option from a repeated delay. |
| 21. Audit a resource on old saves | Balance | Current rates, old values and applicable saves | Milestone and affordability comparison | Test a novice, a stockpiled save and a returning or legacy save at matching opportunities. |
| 22. Design procedural content | Game Design | Generator inputs, reachability guarantee and variation allowed | Invariant, invalid layout and evaluation | A required key beyond the only door requiring the key falsifies reachability. Evaluate variety separately. |
| 23. Keep a record of retired mechanics | Mechanics | Previous placement, decision and rationale | Retired placement and reconsideration trigger | Original evidence remains available and a change in condition can reopen the candidate. |
| 24. Find duplicates | Mechanics | Two exact candidate descriptions and examples | Alias, parameter variant or separate rule decision | A trigger, transition or boundary can disprove equivalence. |
| 25. Review an update | Mechanics | Old and new rule pins plus atlas scope | Affected and uncertain placements and prior checks | A dependent cue opens a recheck. An unrelated placement remains an explicit control. |
| 26. Tailor material to a reader | Game Design | One approved rule and recipient needs | Scoped views for selected recipients | The same starting state and boundary yield one consistent outcome across views. |
| 27. Cover different player groups | Balance | Audience differences and eligible opportunity | Comparable cases and an observation question per group | A measured difference is interpretable within comparable exposure. |
| 28. Review a patch | Balance | Prior forecast, conditions and comparable observations | Cohort and aggregate outcome review | A population-mix change can disagree with outcomes within cohorts. |
| 29. Seek a counterexample | Game Design | Claimed invariant and legal actions | Minimal falsifying sequence or inspected state boundary | A second reader can reproduce the state that violates the claim. |
| 30. Design session exit and return | Game Design | Stop point, saved state and next player decision | Resume and recovery rule with an orientation cue | A resumed player can locate the next decision. Duplicate completion supplies a replay control. |

## Balance family coverage

| Research family | Lead | Minimum input | Output | Independent check |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Target and baseline | Balance | Mode and intended outcome | Baseline measure and guardrail | Repeat comparable exposure. |
| Activity value and cost | Balance | Action, benefit and cost opportunity | Value trace | Change one cost and compare the decision. |
| Chance and rare outcomes | Balance | Eligible draw rule and attempts | Typical result and tail | Compare with a guarantee or dependent draws. |

| Level difficulty and learning | Balance | Stage and eligible attempts | Learning and retry profile | Separate first attempts and practiced trials. |
| Pacing, pressure and respite | Balance | Event timeline and next decision | Exposure and recovery profile | Shift one event and check available action time. |
| Economy and resources | Balance | Sources, sinks, cap and initial stock | Flow and affordability trace | Reconcile typical, adverse and resumed paths. |
| Progression and rewards | Balance | Eligibility and applicable save states | Milestone and choice comparison | Verify migration, cap and replay grant. |
| Synergies and effect order | Balance | Triggers and ordering | State-transition trace | Reverse the order and compare the outcome. |
| Competition and metagame | Balance | Side, initiative, map and experience | Matched strategy comparison | Swap sides and compare across skill strata. |
| Co-op and roles | Balance | Composition, role access and waits | Per-role participation comparison | Remove one actor and trace recovery. |
| Accessibility, controls and assists | Balance | Cue, device and effective input clock | Input and response comparison | Compare threshold and access with a device or participant when required. |
| Simulation and robustness | Balance | Rules, policy and state distribution | Policy-specific sensitivity | Change the policy and test held-out states. |
| Measurement and change validation | Balance | Matching cohorts and eligible exposure | Effect, uncertainty and decision | Compare within groups and inspect raw totals and guardrails. |
